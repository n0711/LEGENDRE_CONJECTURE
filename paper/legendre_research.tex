\begin{abstract}
We audit a triangular-kernel explicit formula for consecutive squares,
document the external status of the 2026 formal RH announcement, and provide
finite computational and formal-verification scaffolding.  No complete proof
of Legendre's conjecture is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Kernel}
Let \(x=n(n+1)\), \(h=\log(1+1/n)\), and
\[
f_n(t)=\sqrt{x/t}\max(0,1-|\log(t/x)|/h).
\]
Its Mellin transform is
\[
F_n(s)=\frac{2x^s(\cosh((s-\tfrac12)h)-1)}
{h(s-\tfrac12)^2}.
\]
The support is \((n(n+1)e^{-h},n(n+1)e^h)=(n^2-1,(n+1)^2)\).

\section{Explicit Formula}
Under RH,
\[
\sum_m\Lambda(m)f_n(m)
=F_n(1)-\sum_\rho F_n(\rho)-\sum_{k\ge1}F_n(-2k).
\]

\section{Remaining Obstacle}
Known RH estimates give prime gaps of size \(O(\sqrt{x}\log x)\), while
Legendre requires control at the \(\sqrt{x}\) scale.  The weighted zero-sum
cancellation condition remains unproved.

\section{Computational Status}
A segmented sieve verified all intervals \(n^2<p<(n+1)^2\) for
\(1\le n\le10^4\).  This is numerical evidence, not an infinite theorem.
